When we moved back to the Midwest and downsized, I sorted my bookshelves and saw what a collection looked like. I had never meant to have one.

It began in 1978 with the Little Black Books, three digest booklets in a black box.

The first time I cast it off, I was putting away childish things. That was the first mistake.

The call to play Traveller brought it back. It is a game I can play within a single mind, and despite work, career, family, and promiscuous attention, I found occasional chances to play it with others. The books came back with it, one at a time.

Some did more than fill a shelf. _SORAG_, from Paranoia Press in 1981, came early. Chuck Kallenbach II’s book on the Zhodani intelligence service expanded what I could put into my game: a template for imagination. Much later, Digest Group Publications’ _World Builders’ Handbook_ of 1989 gave a referee the detail to flesh out a system. DGP gets its own post, _The Trove Beneath the Bridge_.

The second time I cast it off, I was marching toward midlife, and I lost what could not be replaced: printouts of the early internet community’s work and some of the DGP material. That community gets its own post, too.

It came back anyway, the same way it had the first time, and grew to hold most editions of the game. My name is among the playtesters of _The Traveller’s Handbook_, the core book of QuikLink’s _Traveller20_ in 2002. I had no love for d20, but I wanted Traveller in active print.

I only saw how far it had come when I sorted those shelves in the Midwest.
