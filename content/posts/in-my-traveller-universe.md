I started in 1978, with _Dungeons & Dragons_. A first-level warrior leaves his home village knowing the way to the market town and the roads to the next villages. He knows the general direction of the great places beyond, but nothing of how far off they are or what lies between them. So the map grows outward from the village, one road at a time, and it is his own.

A group of Travellers starts out knowing more. They are merchants, scouts, marines and the rest, with years of service behind them. They know their homeworlds and the worlds they have touched, and they carry stories from those years and from the people they met. Beyond that, they have a sense of distance, and data systems to look up whatever they might need.

The Little Black Books gave me what I needed to put flesh on those worlds: to populate them, give them governments and industry, and lay the trade routes between them. Every referee did it their own way.

Then the books supplied a universe of their own. _Mercenary_, in 1978, assumed a remote, centralized government it called the Imperium. A year later _The Kinunir_ set an adventure inside it, and _The Spinward Marches_ mapped its worlds.

I embraced the new setting, and the game moved into it. For many years my Traveller universe was our group’s variant of the Official Traveller Universe. We took turns as referee, and each of us built out the corner where our campaigns ran. We explored the Spinward Marches and fought in the Fifth Frontier War. We ran trade and treason among the worlds of the Core, and fought pirates in the space between there and the Marches. The setting moved on, and we followed it: the civil war, the Virus, the pocket empires and the Reformation Coalition of _The New Era_.

My first attempts at an alternate Traveller universe were hard going, because in my head I was competing with the Official Traveller Universe, by then well developed in all its variant forms. The one that stuck, though I never finished it, was the Bottom of the Hole: a setting at the far end of a wormhole, much like the Clement Sector I found years later. There we built new worlds, away from an Earth history that hasn’t yet occurred.

Some time as an editor on the Traveller wiki led me to Far Home and the Distant Fringe. The work was thrilling and freeing, and it gave me a fresh way to consider what could be. We only ever admired Far Home.
