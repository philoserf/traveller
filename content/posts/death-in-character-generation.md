**Outline. Replace each item with prose.**

1. **Opening: the throw.** A character you have spent twenty minutes on, in the third term of service, and two dice come up short. Book 1 doesn't say the character was wounded. It says the character is dead. Your own first one, if you remember it.

2. **What Book 1 says.** Each four-year term starts with a survival throw on two dice, and failing it ends the character. The survival numbers, one per service:
   - Navy 5+ (DM +2 if Intelligence 7+)
   - Marines 6+ (DM +2 if Endurance 8+)
   - Army 5+ (DM +2 if Education 6+)
   - Scouts 7+ (DM +2 if Endurance 9+)
   - Merchants 5+ (DM +2 if Intelligence 7+)
   - Other 5+ (DM +2 if Intelligence 9+)

   Check these against your copy of the 1977 printing.

3. **The arithmetic.** A 5+ succeeds 83% of the time, a 6+ 72%, and a 7+ 58%. Over five terms that leaves about 40%, 20%, and 7% of characters alive. The Scouts without the DM are the stark case. The DM changes everything: a 5+ becomes a 3+, which succeeds 97% of the time.

4. **Why it is there.** Every reenlistment is a bet. Another term buys another skill, another mustering-out roll, and a higher rank. It also brings another survival throw and, after the fourth term, aging. Without death, the bet has no stakes and every character serves to retirement. Death is also the only thing that ends a career while the dice are still in your hand, so the player is choosing at the table, not just rolling.

5. **What came after (keep this short).** Later printings and _The Traveller Book_ offered an optional rule: a failed survival throw means the character is injured and leaves the service, not dead. Check the wording and the year. Later editions moved further, to mishap tables. One paragraph, pointing to the `edition-*` posts if any are live.

6. **At the table.** What you actually did, and what you'd tell a new referee. Some options to react to:
   - Play it as written. Character generation is quick, so roll another.
   - Use the injury rule from the first session.
   - Let a player stop at any time but give no second chance.
   - Run character generation together, so a death becomes a shared story and the next character knew the dead one.

7. **Close.** One line on what the rule teaches about the game: the universe was dangerous before you arrived in it.
